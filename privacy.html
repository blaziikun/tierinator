Privacy

Tierinator runs in your browser. There's no account, no sign-up, no tracking, no analytics, no ads and no cookies. Nobody behind the site can see your lists.

What's kept, and where

Your lists, the pictures you add, your themes and your settings are saved in your own browser's storage (local storage and IndexedDB) on this site's address, so they're there next time. Nothing is uploaded. They stay until you delete them, or until your browser clears the site's data.

So that it opens without a connection, and can be installed like an app, a copy of Tierinator itself (its pages, code, fonts and icons, plus any templates and packs you open) is also kept on your device. It's the same for everyone and holds nothing about you or your lists, and keeping it sends nothing anywhere. A new version replaces it when you reload.

This storage is strictly necessary for the service you asked for (keeping your lists), so there's no consent banner. Nothing is stored for any other purpose.

Your controls

  • Download everything: File → Back up all lists. One file with every list, picture and setting.
  • Delete everything: File → About & privacy → Delete everything. Removes all of it from this browser at once.

What other sites see

The site is served by Cloudflare, which handles the usual technical information any website receives (such as your IP address) to deliver the pages and protect them from abuse. Some features fetch things straight from other sites, and those sites see your IP address as if you'd visited them yourself:

  • Pokémon sprites: GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com) and jsDelivr, from the PokeAPI, PokeMiners and PMDCollab collections, only while a Pokémon list is open.
  • Extra artwork: the Bulbagarden Archives, when you look at those pictures.
  • Searches you run in Add items: Wikipedia, AniList, Kitsu, Jikan (MyAnimeList), TVmaze, iTunes, Open Library and REST Countries, and any wiki you import from. They receive what you searched for, and the pictures in the results load from their image servers.
  • Pictures in a list: a picture added from a web address keeps loading from that address. In a list file or CSV you open, that's whatever addresses the file holds, so open files only from people you'd take a file from. In a share link, pictures from sites other than the ones above wait until you choose to show them.
  • Fonts: Google Fonts, only for a font other than the three built-in ones: one the list on screen uses (you chose it, or it came in a shared list), or one a saved look in Style uses, to show it. An exported SVG asks for its fonts from Google Fonts too when it's opened, so it looks the same anywhere.
  • Templates: Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org and upload.wikimedia.org), for pictures of a template's items and who made them, when you open one. Importing from Wikipedia or another Wikimedia wiki asks that wiki who made its pictures too. Picture credits link to the picture's page on Wikimedia Commons (or Wikipedia); that site is contacted only if you follow the link.
  • wsrv.nl: an image service, used only to copy a picture from a site that won't allow it directly.

Tierinator sends none of them any identifier of its own, and they're never told which page you came from. Your browser may still send those sites' own cookies, as it would for any picture on the web, depending on its settings.

Share links

A share link carries the list inside the link itself, after the “#”. Browsers never send that part to this site, so it isn't stored anywhere but in the link. Anyone you give the link to can see the list, so don't put anything private in a list you share.

Accessibility

Tierinator aims to meet WCAG 2.2 at level AA. So far:

  • Dragging always has another way: Tab to the tiers or the pool, move between items with the arrow keys, select with Space and press a tier's number, or move an item with Alt and the arrows. Quick rank (press Q) ranks a whole list from the keyboard alone.
  • Buttons, menus and windows work with a keyboard, and focus is always visible. Shortcuts are listed under the keyboard button (or press ?).
  • Controls have names for screen readers, and messages are announced.
  • Text colours are checked against their background and swapped if a theme makes them hard to read.
  • Animation is switched off when your system asks for reduced motion. The site has one dark look; every colour, font and size in it can be changed under Style (lighter backgrounds included).
  • Automatic accessibility checks run on every change before it goes live.

Known gap: pictures are identified by their names rather than described. If something gets in your way, please say so (below) and it will be fixed.
